\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Explaining CNNs and Transformers.} Grad-CAM~\cite{selvaraju2017gradcam} weights the last
convolutional feature maps by class-specific gradients. Attention rollout~\cite{abnar2020attention}
multiplies residual-augmented attention matrices across layers; Chefer \etal~\cite{chefer2021transformer}
note that its output is fixed for a given input regardless of the target class and propose a
class-specific relevance propagation for Transformers. Integrated Gradients~\cite{sundararajan2017axiomatic}
is model-agnostic and class-specific, which makes it a natural shared explainer across architectures.

\textbf{Evaluating explanations.} RISE~\cite{petsiuk2018rise} introduced deletion and insertion curves;
Samek \etal~\cite{samek2017evaluating} proposed region perturbation curves. The pointing
game~\cite{zhang2016topdown} checks whether a heatmap's maximum falls on the annotated object. For medical
imaging, Komorowski \etal~\cite{komorowski2023towards} evaluate Vision Transformer explanations on chest
X-rays by faithfulness, sensitivity and complexity, and show that the choice of criteria changes the
ranking of methods. On HAM10000, Saarela and Geogieva~\cite{saarela2022robustness} compare the fidelity,
stability and robustness of Integrated Gradients and LIME for a skin-lesion classifier.
Kozachok and Seregin~\cite{kozachok2026melanoscope} report IoU between rollout or Grad-CAM maps and expert
annotations for several architectures in a dermoscopy decision-support system.
